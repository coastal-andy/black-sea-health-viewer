# Day 1, first presentation: context (Andrei's notes)

## Context
- Goal: understand the environment of the Black Sea.
- They monitor the **seabed ecosystems**: seagrass, algae, fauna.
- Seagrass meadows strip nitrogen very efficiently, removing the nutrients that cause algal blooms. They work as oxygen pumps: the presenter's figure was **1 m² of seagrass = 10 L of O₂ per day**.
- The Black Sea has few vegetation species, but the ones it has matter: fish nurseries, carbon sequestration, water quality.

## Objectives (of the project / NIMRD)
1. Collect and harmonise data, and use underwater platforms to map the coastal marine environment in selected regions.
2. Collect new data: images and videos.
3. **Digital seabed habitat maps**, with emphasis on **protected marine species**.
4. **Delineate ecologically sensitive regions** and **prioritise potential marine protected areas (MPAs)**.
5. Identify the **threats** that can affect seabed habitats.

## What this tells us
The challenge will most likely be about imagery: AUV/ROV images and video of the seabed turned into habitat maps, with protected habitats highlighted, plus MPA prioritisation and threats. Buoys look like supporting context (water quality, hypoxia) rather than the main thing.

## Background to check with the lecturers (my knowledge, not from the talk)
- Romanian coast vegetation worth naming: seagrass *Zostera noltei* and *Z. marina* (rare, mostly the southern coast around Mangalia, 2 Mai and Vama Veche); brown algae belts formerly called *Cystoseira* (now *Gongolaria barbata* and *Ericaria crinita*); red algae *Phyllophora*.
- Fauna habitats: *Mytilus galloprovincialis* mussel beds (biogenic reef, habitat type 1170), *Modiolula phaseolina* beds on deeper mud.
- Existing protection: Vama Veche–2 Mai marine reserve and several Natura 2000 marine sites along the coast. Ask which sites they surveyed.
- Typical threats: eutrophication and hypoxia (Danube nutrients), bottom trawling and dredging for *Rapana venosa*, invasive species (*Rapana*, *Mnemiopsis*), coastal works and beach nourishment (turbidity, burial), litter and ghost nets, warming.

## Questions to ask at the "uses and analysis" talk
- What data do we get: still images, video, both? Is there a nav log with timestamps (needed to put each image on a map)?
- Are any images already labelled? Which habitat classes do they use (EUNIS? Habitats Directive codes?)
- Which species or habitats count as "protected" for this challenge?
- How do they define "ecologically sensitive" today? Any existing criteria or weights for MPA prioritisation?
- Which threats can be seen in the imagery (litter, trawl marks, invasive species, dead mussels)?
- What output do they want: a map, a report, a tool their staff can reuse?
