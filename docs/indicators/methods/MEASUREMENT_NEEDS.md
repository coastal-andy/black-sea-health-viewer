## 1. New measurement needs

Five yes/no criteria per need, counted (no weights, no composite index; criteria and thresholds OURS):

- **D, sources disagree**: |bias| larger than the variable's typical error, or R2 of the anomalies below 0.3; nutrients: Spearman below 0.5.
- **N, no trusted in-situ sensor** reporting there (trust good or usable, data within 60 days).
- **S, a sensor exists but is not trusted** (flag in `marinekit.datahub`): usually the cheapest fix.
- **T, gap in time**: no trusted source passes the trend gate (8 complete years), or the in-situ record has ended, or there is none.
- **P, decision at stake**: an OFFICIAL-RO or OFFICIAL-EU threshold depends on the variable.

## 2. Uncertainty explained by user level

### Introduction, public

Every value on this site comes from one of three places: a **measurement** (a buoy or a water sample), a **satellite** (sees only the surface), or a **computer model** (fills the gaps). None is perfect. Next to each value we say how far you can trust it: *reliable*, *fairly reliable* or *rough guide only*.

### Introduction, student

Every value comes from a **measurement** (buoy, water sample), a **satellite** (surface only) or a **model** (computed everywhere). To know how wrong a value can be, we compare the sources on the same day at the same place. The typical difference (RMSE) and the average offset (bias) are the uncertainty; where no measurement exists, the uncertainty is unknown, not zero.

### Introduction, stakeholder

Every value comes from a measurement, a satellite or a model, and each has an error. For each indicator we say what that error means for your decision: whether a 'watch' or 'alert' can be acted on directly, or first needs a measurement on site.

### Introduction, researcher

Uncertainty is estimated by matching in-situ, satellite and model daily values at the offshore buoys (EUXRoOB01-03 2024-2026, EUXRo03 2013-2023) and coastal bottle samples (Constanta, 1993-2021), reporting bias, RMSE, unbiased RMSE, R², R² of anomalies, slope and Spearman. Sensors that fail the check are flagged in `marinekit.datahub` and never used as the automatic source. Seasonal cycles and trends are shown only behind a quality gate. Thresholds labelled OURS are our proposals; all uncertainties are indicative (point values, few stations).

**public**

**Reliable.** The satellite temperature is usually within about half a degree (0.45 °C) of what the buoys measure.

**student**

**Reliable.** The satellite temperature is usually within about half a degree (0.45 °C) of what the buoys measure.

**Two numbers describe the error.** *RMSE* (root mean square error) is the typical size of the difference between two sources on one day. *Bias* is the average difference: a constant offset that could be corrected. For temperature the satellite differs from the buoys by about 0.43 °C on a typical day (RMSE, 5458 matched days at 4 offshore buoys) and reads 0.07 °C cooler on average (bias).

**Why a high R² can mislead.** R² (how much of the variation one source explains in the other) is 0.996-0.996, almost perfect, but most of it is the seasons that both sources share: winter cold and summer warm. With the monthly means removed, R² drops to 0.90-0.92. Agreement is about RMSE and bias, not only R².

**Rough guide only.** The map shows where there is more or less algae, but the exact amount can be off by half or double. Buoy readings are not used because they read 2 to 3 times too high.

**Two numbers describe the error.** *RMSE* (root mean square error) is the typical size of the difference between two sources on one day. *Bias* is the average difference: a constant offset that could be corrected. Chlorophyll spans more than 100 times from clear to bloom water, so errors are computed on a logarithmic scale: a difference of 0.2 in log10 means a factor of 10^0.2, about 1.6. **Agreement is not accuracy.** Two sources that agree can both be wrong; here no trusted measurement exists offshore to check either, so the true error is not known.
